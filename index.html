AI Auditor: document compliance for AI-readiness

A multi-agent system that audits document repositories before they reach a RAG pipeline.

Feed a RAG system stale or non-compliant documents and it repeats their problems with full confidence. AI Auditor checks every file first, scoring it on staleness, standards compliance and governance metadata to produce a trust score from 0 to 1, and flags anything below 0.70. A LangGraph supervisor then routes specialist agents — researcher, coder, auditor, general — to fix what the audit finds. The LLM layer is provider-agnostic: fully local on Ollama, or Anthropic, Groq and OpenAI by changing one line of config.

Under the hood: a deterministic audit engine with zero LLM calls, ReAct specialists, an optional Reflexion self-critique step, a plan-and-execute planner with parallel fan-out (LangGraph Send API), human-in-the-loop approval through interrupt(), agentic RAG (CRAG relevance grading, query rewrite, cross-encoder reranking, structured citations), pluggable episodic memory (Mem0 or LangMem), guardrails (encoding-aware prompt-injection detection, PII redaction, RBAC, rate limiting), MCP tools over stdio and HTTP/SSE, and a full eval suite. One Docker Compose command, optional Postgres checkpointer, Langfuse for tracing.

Behavioural risk scoring for financial transactions

A hybrid fraud risk-scoring framework, built to survive a model-validation review.

Fraud is a rare-event problem. On PaySim only about 0.13% of transactions are fraudulent, so overall accuracy means little, and a false alarm costs nothing like a missed fraud. The framework has three layers. Detection combines a supervised XGBoost model with an unsupervised Isolation Forest into a single risk score. Decision maps that score to four bands, with the cutoff set to minimise expected cost. Explanation adds calibrated probabilities and per-transaction SHAP reasons that an investigator — or a customer under GDPR Article 22 — can actually be shown.

On method: the train/test split happens before any preprocessing, so there is no leakage, and leakage-prone features are removed. PR-AUC is the headline metric, since accuracy is misleading at this imbalance. I benchmarked 12 models — logistic regression through Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost — plus anomaly detectors (Isolation Forest, OCSVM, LOF, ECOD, COPOD, autoencoder). The figure I report is the ablated PR-AUC of 0.97, not the 0.998 the simulator inflates through its fixed accounting pattern. The design follows FINMA validation expectations, Swiss FADP and GDPR Article 22, and the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations, and ships with a FastAPI scoring prototype.

Cell tower coverage in Egypt

Egypt's mobile network mapped at tower level, across four operators and four radio generations.

A geospatial analysis of mobile infrastructure in Egypt, with a closer look at coverage gaps along the Nile corridor. It uses OpenCellID data (MCC 602), broken down by operator and radio family (GSM, UMTS, LTE, NR). Interactive Leaflet maps show where each operator is concentrated, how the radio generations roll out, the coverage radius of each tower, and the stretches near the river with no coverage at all. It started as a side project linking my day job in maritime connectivity with geospatial data, and it is upfront about the limits of crowdsourced data: counts are a lower bound, biased toward urban areas.
