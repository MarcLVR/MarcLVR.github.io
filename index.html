Agentic AI · Governance · Responsible AI

AI Auditor: Document Compliance for AI-Readiness

Python · LangGraph

A multi-agent system that audits document repositories before they reach a RAG pipeline.

If you feed a RAG system stale or non-compliant documents, it repeats their problems with full confidence. AI Auditor checks every file first, scoring it on staleness, standards compliance, and governance metadata to produce a trust score from 0 to 1, and flags anything below 0.70. A LangGraph supervisor then routes specialist agents (researcher, coder, auditor, general) to help fix what the audit finds. The LLM layer is provider-agnostic: it runs fully local on Ollama, or connects to Anthropic, Groq, or OpenAI by changing one line of config.

Under the hood: a deterministic audit engine (zero LLM calls), ReAct specialists, an optional Reflexion self-critique step, a plan-and-execute planner with parallel fan-out (LangGraph Send API), human-in-the-loop approval through interrupt(), agentic RAG (CRAG relevance grading, query rewrite, cross-encoder reranking, structured citations), pluggable episodic memory (Mem0 or LangMem), guardrails (encoding-aware prompt-injection detection, PII redaction, RBAC, rate limiting), MCP tools over stdio and HTTP/SSE, and a full eval suite (routing, trajectory and tool-call checks via agentevals, RAGAS). It runs with a single Docker Compose command and an optional Postgres checkpointer, with Langfuse for tracing.

Behavioural Risk Scoring for Financial Transactions

Python · XGBoost · SHAP

A hybrid fraud risk-scoring framework (UEBA-inspired), built to pass a model-validation review.

Fraud is a rare-event problem. On PaySim, only about 0.13% of transactions are fraudulent, so overall accuracy means little, and the cost of a false alarm is very different from the cost of a missed fraud. The framework has three layers. Detection combines a supervised XGBoost model with an unsupervised Isolation Forest into one risk score. Decision maps that score to four bands (allow, monitor, review, block), with the cutoff set to minimise expected cost rather than left at the usual 0.5. Explanation adds calibrated probabilities and per-transaction SHAP reasons that an investigator, or a customer under GDPR Article 22, can actually be shown.

On method: the train/test split happens before any preprocessing, so there is no leakage, and leakage-prone features are removed. PR-AUC is the main metric, since accuracy is misleading at this level of imbalance. I compared 12 models, from logistic regression to Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost, plus anomaly detectors (Isolation Forest, OCSVM, LOF, ECOD, COPOD, and an autoencoder). The figure I report is the ablated PR-AUC of 0.97, not the 0.998 the simulator inflates through its fixed accounting pattern. The design follows FINMA validation expectations, Swiss FADP and GDPR Article 22, and the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations, and it ships with a FastAPI scoring prototype.

Geospatial Analytics · Data Visualisation

Cell Tower Coverage in Egypt: Operators & Radio Technology

R · Quarto · Leaflet

Egypt's mobile network mapped at tower level, across four operators and four radio generations.

A geospatial analysis of mobile infrastructure in Egypt, with a closer look at coverage gaps along the Nile corridor from Cairo to Aswan. It uses OpenCellID data (MCC 602), broken down by operator (Etisalat, Orange, Vodafone, WE) and radio family (GSM, UMTS, LTE, NR). Interactive Leaflet maps show where each operator is concentrated, how the radio generations roll out, the coverage radius of each tower, and the areas near the river with no coverage at all. It started as a side project linking my day job in telecom (at a maritime-connectivity provider) with geospatial data, and it is upfront about the limits of crowdsourced data: counts are a lower bound, with a bias toward urban areas.
